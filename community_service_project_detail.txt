1. PROJECT OVERVIEW
==================================================

CareerPath Navigator is a comprehensive web application designed to bridge the career awareness gap for students, particularly in rural areas. The project addresses the critical issue where talented students limit their aspirations due to lack of exposure to diverse career opportunities. Through this digital platform, students can explore various career paths, understand educational requirements, and make informed decisions about their future.

The initiative was inspired by a visit to a rural high school where students were primarily interested in careers they had seen in their community or on television, revealing a significant knowledge gap. A particularly memorable moment involved a gifted mathematics student who had never considered careers in data science or actuarial studies simply because she had never heard of them.

==================================================
2. COMMUNITY IMPACT & SOCIAL PURPOSE
==================================================

TARGET BENEFICIARIES:
- Students in rural and underserved communities
- Parents with limited educational backgrounds seeking to guide their children
- Career counselors and educators in schools with limited resources
- Young adults seeking career transition guidance

COMMUNITY PARTNER:
G. Koteshwar Rao, a 43-year-old auto driver and father of two school-going children, represents the target community. Despite his dedication to his children's education, he struggles to guide them through career options due to his limited educational background. His story reflects the common challenge faced by many parents in rural villages who wish for better tools and resources to help their children navigate career paths.

SOCIAL PROBLEMS ADDRESSED:
1. Limited Career Awareness: Students in rural areas are exposed to only a few career options visible in their immediate environment
2. Educational Pathway Confusion: Lack of clear guidance on educational requirements for different careers
3. Technology Underutilization: While students have smartphones, they're primarily used for entertainment rather than educational purposes
4. Resource Accessibility: Limited access to career counseling and guidance services
